Healthy Porridge with Ceylon Cinnamon

Fair warning: this is not an IT post. Not everything has to be IT. Even a server needs a human who has eaten something, and my uptime depends on breakfast. Consider this a scheduled maintenance window for my stomach. The blog's main subject will return shortly.

The base is simple. Put rolled oats in a pot with water, or milk if you like it creamy, add a pinch of salt and cook on low heat, stirring, until it is thick and soft. About 5 to 10 minutes. No sugar needed.

My upgrade is the cinnamon. I use Ceylon cinnamon instead of the cheapest ground cinnamon on the supermarket shelf. It usually costs a little more, and a jar lasts a long time because you only use a pinch. The real reason is the coumarin. Cassia cinnamon, which many cheap jars are, contains much more coumarin than Ceylon, and German health authorities advise against eating a lot of cassia every day. Ceylon has only low levels. Check the label: if it does not say Ceylon, it is probably cassia. I am not claiming porridge cures anything, and the evidence for cinnamon as a medicine is weak. But it is a safer habit for a daily pinch, and it tastes amazing, warmer and a bit sweeter and more delicate.

Once the porridge is cooked, add the fruit on top: sliced banana, and either kaki (persimmon) or a chopped apple. The warm porridge softens the banana, the kaki is sweet and juicy, and the apple gives a little crunch. It is a good mix of taste and fibre, and you don't need to add sugar.
